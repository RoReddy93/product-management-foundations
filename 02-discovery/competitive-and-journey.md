# Competitive Analysis & Journey Map (Module 2)

## Responses
- **Role, who are you solving for? (the specific user segment or profile):** A discerning film enthusiast who values cinematic quality, diverse storytelling, and trusted editorial recommendations over content volume.
- **Goal, what is this user ultimately trying to achieve?:** Discover exceptional films and hidden gems that reflect their nuanced tastes through thoughtful, credible curation.
- **Friction, the main barrier (moment of misery) stopping them from succeeding:** Receives repetitive franchise-based recommendations that fail to reflect their interests, ultimately losing trust in StreamLine's discovery experience and potentially switching to specialized competitors offering human-curated selections.
- **External tools, the outside platforms or tools the user is forced to use:** Google, Letterboxd, IMDb, Reddit, recommendations from friends, film critics, and curated newsletters from specialized streaming competitors.
- **The process, the 3 to 5 manual steps the user takes to get the job done:** 1. Browse StreamLine's homepage and encounter repetitive, irrelevant recommendations.
2. Leave StreamLine to search external platforms or seek trusted human recommendations.
3. Shortlist films based on reviews, editorial recommendations, and personal interests.
4. Return to StreamLine and manually check whether the shortlisted films are available.
5. Switch to a competing streaming service if StreamLine cannot meet their needs.
- **Core frustration, the exact moment the process feels most “broken”:** Despite paying for a streaming service with over 15,000 titles, the user must leave the platform to discover something worth watching. This fragmented experience transfers customer trust and engagement to external platforms, ultimately increasing the risk of subscriber churn and weakening StreamLine's competitive position.
- **The evidence, a specific quote or behavior from the research that proves this:** UXR-04 — Tom, lapsed subscriber:
"I cancelled last year. It became a warehouse. Volume went up, quality of my evenings went down. A competitor emails me two hand-picked films a week and I watch both."

UXR-02 — Marcus, film enthusiast:
"I'm not a genre, I'm a person. I miss when a human curator would surface a hidden gem."
